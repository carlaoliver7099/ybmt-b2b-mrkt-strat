Why your pool needs a proper deck.

A pool without a quality deck is an unfinished space. Here are the five things a properly built pool deck delivers:

  • SAFETY — slip-rated surfaces around water reduce risk of serious injury, especially with children and aging family members.
  • COMPLIANCE — a properly engineered deck integrates seamlessly with QLD pool fencing requirements (more on this in Chapter 4).
  • LIFESTYLE — transforms an ordinary backyard into a resort-quality entertaining space you'll actually use.
  • PROPERTY VALUE — pool decks add measurable resale value, especially in premium SEQ markets like Noosa, Mermaid Beach and Bulimba.
  • LONGEVITY — protects the pool surround from weather, foot traffic and chemical splash, extending the life of your pool investment.

CHAPTER 02

Materials decoded.

The single biggest decision you'll make is what to build with. Each material has trade-offs. Here's the honest breakdown.

COMPOSITE DECKING (Trex, Ezy Decking, NewTechWood)

  • Price: $350–$420/m² installed in SEQ
  • Lifespan: 25–30+ years
  • Maintenance: Minimal — occasional wash
  • Slip rating: Excellent (textured surface)
  • Pros: Won't warp/splinter, chlorine-resistant, 20-yr warranty common, modern aesthetic
  • Cons: Higher upfront cost, can heat up in direct QLD sun (choose lighter tones)
  • Best for: Busy families wanting low maintenance, modern coastal homes

SPOTTED GUM HARDWOOD

  • Price: $320–$380/m² installed
  • Lifespan: 25+ years with maintenance
  • Maintenance: Annual oiling required
  • Slip rating: Good when properly textured
  • Pros: Premium Australian aesthetic, beautiful grain, ages gracefully, sustainable
  • Cons: Requires upkeep, can splinter if neglected
  • Best for: Premium architectural homes, Hamptons/coastal styles

Materials decoded — continued.

MERBAU HARDWOOD

  • Price: $240–$310/m² installed
  • Lifespan: 20+ years with maintenance
  • Maintenance: Annual oiling required
  • Slip rating: Good
  • Pros: Warm rich tone, cost-effective hardwood option, classic look
  • Cons: Tannin bleeding initially, sourcing concerns (always choose certified sustainable)
  • Best for: Budget-conscious homeowners wanting a premium look

TRAVERTINE PAVERS

  • Price: $400–$500+/m² installed
  • Lifespan: 50+ years
  • Maintenance: Sealing every few years
  • Slip rating: Excellent (naturally porous, cool underfoot)
  • Pros: Luxury aesthetic, stays cool, doesn't fade, premium feel
  • Cons: Higher cost, requires proper sub-base, can stain if unsealed
  • Best for: High-end luxury homes, Mediterranean/resort styles

CHAPTER 04

QLD pool safety compliance, simplified.

This chapter alone could save you tens of thousands in retrofit costs. The Queensland Development Code MP 3.4 sets strict rules — most homeowners (and many builders) get them wrong.

✓
FENCE HEIGHT — minimum 1.2m (1200mm) measured from ground level on the outside of the barrier.
✓
NON-CLIMBABLE ZONE — no climbable objects (furniture, planters, deck balustrades) within 900mm of the outside of the fence.
✓
FENCE GAPS — no gaps in the barrier greater than 100mm.
✓
GATES — self-closing, self-latching only, opening AWAY from the pool.
✓
DECK HEIGHT RULE — if a deck sits less than 1800mm above ground AND provides direct access to the pool area, special compliance requirements apply.
✓
DECK-AS-FENCE — if your deck edge forms part of the pool barrier, that edge must comply with fence height and non-climb rules.
✓
POOL SAFETY CERTIFICATE — required to verify compliance (and required at sale of property).

Common compliance traps.

01
The furniture trap
Placing outdoor lounges, BBQs or pot plants within 900mm of the pool fence — automatic climbable object violation at inspection.
02
The planter trap
Large pots or built-in planters near fence lines automatically fail inspection — they're seen as climb aids for small children.
03
The retrofit trap
Building the deck first and trying to retrofit fencing later is typically 3–4× the cost — and often requires partial demolition.
04
The handover trap
Buying or selling a property without a current Pool Safety Certificate — a contract-breaking issue at settlement.

CHAPTER 05

The 5 most common pool deck mistakes.

01
Ignoring drainage
Water pooling under the deck means a rotted substructure within 3–5 years.
02
Wrong material for the environment
Chlorine, salt and UV will destroy poorly-specified materials within a single Queensland summer.
03
Compliance retrofits
Building first and fixing fencing compliance later is one of the most expensive mistakes a homeowner can make.
04
No expansion gaps
The Queensland heat causes movement — tight installs warp, buckle and split within two seasons.
05
Cheap substructure
Gorgeous boards on bad bones = early failure. The frame matters more than the finish.

What to look for in a pool deck builder.

Before signing anything, run your shortlist through this 10-point checklist. If they can't tick every box, keep looking.

  • QBCC licence — verify online at qbcc.qld.gov.au (non-negotiable).
  • Public liability insurance — $20M+ minimum.
  • Specialization in pool decks — not just general carpentry.
  • Demonstrated experience with pool fence compliance.
  • Written workmanship warranty — in their contract, not verbal.
  • Material warranties passed through to you in writing.
  • Detailed written quote with line-item breakdown.
  • Project timeline committed in writing.
  • Recent references in your area (and willingness to share them).
  • Permits and council approvals handled by them, not you.
